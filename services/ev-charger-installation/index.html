EV charger installation in Norfolk.

Choosing between an exterior wall and a driveway post for a charger in Norfolk changes the cable route, not only the look. Where the post stands away from the buildings, the route and its protection need assessment.

The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your Norfolk property

Sketch the parking spot, the wall or post position and the path a cable would take to the panel, noting any outbuilding, gravel or lawn it crosses. Record whether lights dim when large loads already run.

Ask the contractor whether the charger would share the supply with other large loads, how the route will be protected outdoors, and which existing observations suggest the supply, not the wiring, sets the limit.

What should the assessment establish?

The scope for a charger on a Norfolk wall or driveway post is what the homeowner agrees to or turns down. Named in it are the charger, the circuit that feeds it and the ESA notification involved. It gives the contractor's finding on whether the existing supply can carry the new load next to what already runs. The cable route to the chosen spot is described, with its outdoor protection. The contractor says whether any dimming or flicker already seen points to the supply or to wiring inside, ahead of a new load. Who confirms any supply-side step is named as well.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Does a post away from the house need a different cable route?

It can. A post standing apart from the buildings may need a longer route with its own protection, and that changes the assessment. Describe the distance and what the route crosses so the contractor can scope it.

Why might lights flicker when a vehicle is charging?

Flicker while charging can come from the supply, from a shared circuit or from a loose connection, and a guess is not a diagnosis. Stop using the equipment if it shows heat, a smell or buzzing, and ask a contractor to assess it.
