Panel and service upgrades in Norfolk.

A panel in a Norfolk property may have spare positions and still be asked to carry more than the supply allows. If a shop or barn adds a large load, dimming elsewhere can raise a question about supply, not space.

A panel and an electrical service are different parts of the system

The panel distributes electricity to property circuits. The service is the incoming supply and associated equipment. Replacing a panel does not automatically increase the available supply. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess existing condition and demand, planned loads and the utility requirements before recommending equipment. Spare breaker spaces alone do not establish capacity.

Prepare the scope for your Norfolk property

List the large loads already running and any planned, including equipment in outbuildings, and note which lights dim when they start. Record where the meter and the main panel sit and how far any outbuilding is from them.

Ask the contractor to say if the proposal creates room for more circuits, enlarges what the incoming supply can deliver, or does both, and which of your observations it explains. Ask who contacts the utility if the supply side is involved.

What should the assessment establish?

Too few spare breaker slots and a limit in the incoming supply are treated as two separate findings when dimming follows large loads at a Norfolk property, since extra slots make room for circuits while the supply stays as it was. For each, the contractor sets out what was observed, what was inspected and what the conclusion rests on. The scope names the planned loads it covers, including any in outbuildings, and everything left unassessed, so a later addition is not assumed to fit. Whether the utility has to be involved is answered there too.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can a full panel be the reason my lights dim?

Not necessarily. Spare positions and supply capacity are separate questions, and dimming can come from either the supply or the wiring. Ask the contractor to assess which applies before any panel is replaced.

Does a larger supply from the utility mean the panel must change too?

Often it does. The panel and its main disconnect must be rated for the supply they receive, so a larger service usually means the panel is reviewed too. Ask the contractor to explain in writing which equipment changes and why.

Prepare your enquiry

Useful project details

  • Reason for the upgrade and known concerns
  • Existing major loads and proposed additions
  • Panel information visible without removing covers
  • Equipment location and access restrictions
  • Preferred timing and equipment that must remain operating

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
