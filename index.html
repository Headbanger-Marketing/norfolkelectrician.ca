Before work begins

Sort supply problems from internal faults before calling.

Does your property have a barn, shed or shop? A flicker in one building may not reach the others, so note which lost power, which stayed lit and whether neighbours noticed. In a single house, rooms can be noted the same way.

Write down the time, the duration and what was running when it happened. A contractor can use that record to judge whether to start with the incoming supply, which the utility handles, or with the wiring inside the property.

How can I tell whether a power problem came from outside the property?

Compare notes before touching anything: did the whole property go dark, or only some circuits, and did the neighbours lose power too? Share those observations with the contractor, who can assess the cause and tell you whether the utility needs to be contacted.

Should a quote name the fault or only list the parts it replaces?

A useful quote says what was found and why the work addresses it. Ask the contractor to separate any finding about the incoming supply from findings inside the property, so you can compare quotes on the same basis.

What should I record when the lights dim or flicker?

Note the date, the time, which buildings and rooms were affected and what equipment had just started. Do not remove any cover to take photographs. A written log helps a contractor decide where to look first.

Who has to be involved if the problem is on the supply side?

If the cause lies in the incoming supply, the utility may need to act, and the contractor can explain where its equipment ends and the property's begins. Ask who can authorize access to the meter and the buildings.
